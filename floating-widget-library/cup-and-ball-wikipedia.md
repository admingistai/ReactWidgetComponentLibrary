Cup-and-ball

**Cup-and-ball** (or **ball in a cup**) or **ring and pin** is a traditional children's toy. It is generally a wooden handle to which a small ball is attached by a string and that has one or two cups, [or a spike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendama "Kendama"), upon which the player tries to catch the ball. It is popular in [Spanish-speaking countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_territories_where_Spanish_is_an_official_language "List of countries and territories where Spanish is an official language"), where it is called by a wide number of names (including **boliche** in Spain, **Capirucho** in [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador "El Salvador") and **balero** in most of [Hispanic America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_America "Hispanic America")), and was historically popular in France as the **bilboquet**. A similar toy with three cups and a spike called _[kendama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendama "Kendama")_ is very popular in Japan and has spread globally in popularity.

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cup-and-ball&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

The game was created in the 14th century and has been improved in different ways since then.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### Americas

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cup-and-ball&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Americas")\]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/TianguisDomingoRamos2015_067.JPG/250px-TianguisDomingoRamos2015_067.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:TianguisDomingoRamos2015_067.JPG) Baleros at a [tianguis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianguis "Tianguis") in [Uruapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruapan "Uruapan"), [Michoacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michoacan "Michoacan"), Mexico[Play video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Balero_Demonstration_showing_a_common_method_of_landing_the_cup_on_the_stick.webm "Play video")Balero demonstration in Mexico showing a common technique of landing the cup on the stick.

In North America it was both a child's toy and a gambling mechanism for adults, and involved catching a ring rather than a ball. In some Native American tribes it was even a courtship device, where suitors would challenge the objects of their interest to a polite game of ring and pin.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] The [Mohave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohave_people "Mohave people") variant of the game included up to 17 extra rings attached to the cord, and game scoring involved differing point values assigned to different rings.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-and-ball#cite_note-Leibs-1) Other variants include those played by the [Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit "Inuit") of what is now [Labrador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador "Labrador"), with a rabbit's skull in place of the ball, with extra holes bored into it, which had to be caught on the handle like a skewer; and those that used balls of grass or animal hair.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-and-ball#cite_note-Leibs-1) Ring and pin games in general were known as ᐊᔭᒃ _ajagak_, _ayagak_, and ᐊᔭᖁᒃᑐᒃ _ajaquktuk_ in [Inuit dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit_languages "Inuit languages").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-and-ball#cite_note-Blanchard1995-2)

### France

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cup-and-ball&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: France")\]

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/Bole_Jeanne_L_Enfant_Au_Bilboquet.jpg/250px-Bole_Jeanne_L_Enfant_Au_Bilboquet.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bole_Jeanne_L_Enfant_Au_Bilboquet.jpg) Jeanne Bôle's _L'Enfant au Bilboquet_ (around 1880)

The cup-and-ball is noted in France as early as the sixteenth century.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-and-ball#cite_note-Leibs-1) The game was played by King [Henry III of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_III_of_France "Henry III of France") as historical records note, though his playing was considered evidence of his mental instability.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-and-ball#cite_note-Freer1888-3) After his death, the game went out of fashion, and for a century the game was only remembered by a small number of enthusiasts\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] such as the Marquis de Bièvre.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-and-ball#cite_note-4)

The game had its golden age during the reign of [Louis XV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XV_of_France "Louis XV of France") — among the upper classes people owned baleros made of ivory. Actors also sometimes appeared with them in scenes. The game was very popular in the 18th and 19th centuries. [Jean-Jacques Rousseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Jacques_Rousseau "Jean-Jacques Rousseau") mentions the game early in his _[Confessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessions_(Jean-Jacques_Rousseau) "Confessions (Jean-Jacques Rousseau)")_ when stating his reservations about idle talk and hands, saying "If ever I went back into society I should carry a cup-and-ball in my pocket, and play with it all day long to excuse myself from speaking when I had nothing to say."[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-and-ball#cite_note-5)

### Iberian world

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cup-and-ball&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Iberian world")\]

The game is very popular in the Spanish and Portuguese diaspora. The name varies across many countries — in [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador "El Salvador") and [Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala "Guatemala") it is called **capirucho**; in [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina "Argentina"), [Ecuador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuador "Ecuador"), [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia "Colombia"), [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico "Mexico"), and [Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay "Uruguay") it is called **balero**; in [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain "Spain") it is **boliche**; in [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal "Portugal") and [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil "Brazil") it is called **bilboquê**; in [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile "Chile") it is **emboque**; in [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia "Colombia") it is called **coca**; and in [Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuela "Venezuela") the game is called **perinola**.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-and-ball#cite_note-CIVILA-6)

In 1960, American lexicographer Charles Keilus (1919-1997) documented the term _zingo paya_ for a cup-and-ball game in Tijuana, Mexico, and formed the Zingo Paya Society in Los Angeles to promote the toy and its collection.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-and-ball#cite_note-7)\[ _[importance?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:What_Wikipedia_is_not#Encyclopedic_content "Wikipedia:What Wikipedia is not")_\]

### England

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cup-and-ball&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: England")\]

This game was also popular in England during the early 19th century. [Jane Austen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Austen "Jane Austen") is reputed to have excelled while entertaining her nephew George in a game called _Bilbo Catcher_. In a letter to her sister [Cassandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassandra_Austen "Cassandra Austen") she describes how George is "indefatigable" at the game. Her other nephew, James Edward Austen-Leigh, later praised Austen's own skills at the game, saying "she has been known to catch it on the point above a hundred times in succession, til her hand was weary".[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-and-ball#cite_note-8)

There is one picture in the [National Portrait Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Portrait_Gallery,_London "National Portrait Gallery, London") of a young girl playing the game. It appears to be a copy of a painting by [Philippe Mercier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Mercier "Philippe Mercier"), although the original painting has not been found.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-and-ball#cite_note-9) Unlike other 18th-century toys, which are found repeatedly in artwork, cup-and-ball games are rare, with only two pictures known, one copied from the other.

### Japan

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cup-and-ball&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Japan")\]

The game of [kendama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendama "Kendama") is believed to have arrived in Japan around the middle [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period "Edo period") in the 18th century,[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-and-ball#cite_note-:2-10) and the game underwent significant modernization and standardization in the early 20th century, becoming internationally popular in the 21st century.

### Germany

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cup-and-ball&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Germany")\]

In 2011, a German company, TicToys, began to create a toy with the name Ticayo.

## Gameplay

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cup-and-ball&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Gameplay")\]

The main goal of the game is to get the ball into the cup. While the concept is very easy, mastering the game can sometimes be challenging.

There are several styles of gameplay in the Latin world such as _la simple_, _la doble_, _la vertical_, and _la mariquita_.
